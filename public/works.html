The Larger Work

Children of Crisis was the trunk; the rest of the shelf is its branches. Across more than fifty books, Coles returned again and again to a single question — how does a human being come to have a conscience? — and answered it through children, through teachers, through saints.

The great trilogy

The inner lives of children

If Children of Crisis asked how circumstance shapes a child, the trilogy that grew out of it asked something deeper: what, beneath circumstance, is a child's inner life actually made of? Drawing on decades of interviews with children across the United States and around the world — in Brazil, South Africa, Northern Ireland, Poland — Coles listened past the surface to the moral, political, and spiritual reasoning children do entirely on their own.

1986

The Moral Life of Children

How children form a conscience — their startling capacity for moral seriousness, courage, and ethical struggle, often outpacing the adults around them.

1986

The Political Life of Children

How children absorb nation, power, class, and conflict — what a child in Belfast or Soweto or Boston already understands about the political world.

1990

The Spiritual Life of Children

The questions children ask about God, death, and meaning — taken seriously, across faiths, as genuine theology rather than charming naivety.

Coles continued the thread for younger readers and parents with The Moral Intelligence of Children (1997), a practical, widely read distillation of what his fieldwork had taught him about raising a child of good character.

Teaching & service

Books about how to live

From his Harvard classroom came two of his most beloved books — arguments, really, for the moral uses of literature and of service. They are the closest Coles came to a credo.

  • The Call of Stories 1989 · subtitled "Teaching and the Moral Imagination"

    Drawn from his famous Harvard course, a case for reading novels as moral instruction — how Tolstoy, Chekhov, O'Connor, and Williams teach us to imagine other lives and so to live our own more thoughtfully.

  • The Call of Service 1993 · subtitled "A Witness to Idealism"

    On the moral life of those who give themselves to others — volunteers, activists, teachers — and the satisfactions and temptations of idealism. A book shaped by, and dedicated to, his work with the young.

Lives that taught him

Portraits & biographies

Coles paid his debts in print. The thinkers and writers who formed his conscience — analysts, novelists, activists, mystics — each received a study written in gratitude and close attention.

  • Erik H. Erikson: The Growth of His Work1970

    His study of the mentor who gave his fieldwork its intellectual home.

  • Walker Percy: An American Search1978

    On the novelist-physician whose Southern, searching Catholicism Coles deeply admired.

  • Flannery O'Connor's South1980

    A reading of the writer whose unsparing moral vision he taught for decades.

  • Dorothy Day: A Radical Devotion1987

    The Catholic Worker founder he claimed as a spiritual mother, drawn from their long acquaintance.

  • Simone Weil: A Modern Pilgrimage1987

    On the philosopher-mystic whose attention to affliction mirrored his own.

  • Anna Freud: The Dream of Psychoanalysis1992

    A tribute to the pioneer of child analysis who encouraged his early work.

  • The Old Ones of New Mexico · The Last and First Eskimos1973 · 1978

    Documentary collaborations with photographers — among many — that paired his interviews with images of the people he met.

For young readers

The Story of Ruby Bridges

In 1995, thirty-five years after he first saw her, Coles wrote the picture book that returned Ruby Bridges to the children of a new generation. Illustrated by George Ford, The Story of Ruby Bridges tells, simply and movingly, of the six-year-old who walked alone into a hostile school and prayed for the crowd that taunted her. It remains a fixture of American classrooms — the moment that began his life's work, given back to the kind of reader who could understand it best.

We are all of us pilgrims, on a journey toward an understanding of what our lives mean, what they are for. Robert Coles
